# 11_AI_GOVERNANCE

Version: 1.1
Status: Draft
Document Type: AI Architecture
Priority: Critical

---

# Purpose

This document defines the Artificial Intelligence Governance Architecture of Life Community OS.

Governance ensures that Artificial Intelligence remains controlled, predictable, secure and aligned with the platform architecture throughout its lifecycle.

Artificial Intelligence evolves.

Governance protects that evolution.

---

# Question this document answers

> How is Artificial Intelligence governed throughout the platform?

---

# Scope

This document defines:

- AI governance;
- ownership;
- lifecycle management;
- approval;
- versioning;
- operational responsibility.

It does not define:

- AI providers;
- AI models;
- prompts;
- infrastructure.

---

# Definition

AI Governance defines how AI capabilities are created, approved, versioned, maintained and retired.

Governance protects the long-term quality of the AI Platform.

---

# Objectives

AI Governance exists to:

- preserve architectural consistency;
- maintain Automation-First principles;
- reduce operational risk;
- support continuous evolution;
- protect business integrity;
- ensure responsible AI usage.

---

# Governance Philosophy

Artificial Intelligence is a Core Platform capability.

It is never an isolated subsystem.

Every AI capability evolves according to explicit architectural principles.

---

# Automation-First Governance

Automation remains the primary execution mechanism.

Artificial Intelligence is governed as an optional platform capability.

Governance must always preserve the following execution order:

Business Rules

↓

Automation

↓

Artificial Intelligence

↓

Human Review (when required)

No governance decision may violate this principle.

---

# Ownership

Every AI Capability must have an explicit owner.

Ownership includes responsibility for:

- correctness;
- maintenance;
- documentation;
- monitoring;
- lifecycle;
- continuous improvement.

No AI capability should become ownerless.

---

# Capability Lifecycle

Every AI capability follows an explicit lifecycle.

Draft

↓

Review

↓

Approved

↓

Available

↓

Deprecated

↓

Archived

Lifecycle transitions remain observable.

---

# Versioning

Every AI capability supports versioning.

Historical executions remain linked to the capability version that produced them.

Versioning preserves execution traceability.

---

# Review Process

Before production, AI capabilities should be reviewed for:

- architectural consistency;
- deterministic behaviour;
- security;
- privacy;
- observability;
- performance;
- provider independence.

Major capabilities require architectural review.

---

# Provider Governance

Providers remain implementation details.

Provider replacement should never require changes to:

- Business Domains;
- Automation;
- AI Services;
- AI Capabilities.

Governance preserves provider independence.

---

# Model Governance

Models evolve independently.

Capabilities remain stable.

Model changes should never alter platform behaviour.

---

# Prompt Governance

Prompt engineering belongs to the AI Platform.

Business Domains never own prompts.

Prompt evolution remains centralized.

---

# Context Governance

The platform governs:

- Context construction;
- Context validation;
- Context ownership;
- Context security.

Artificial Intelligence never governs Context.

---

# Memory Governance

The platform governs:

- Memory creation;
- Memory ownership;
- Memory retention;
- Memory deletion;
- Memory auditing.

Artificial Intelligence never governs Memory.

---

# Security Governance

Every AI capability must respect:

- permissions;
- tenant isolation;
- privacy;
- security policies.

Security governance belongs to the platform.

---

# Observability Governance

Every AI execution remains:

- observable;
- traceable;
- auditable;
- explainable.

Invisible AI is not permitted.

---

# Documentation

Every AI capability should remain documented.

Documentation should explain:

- purpose;
- capability;
- responsibilities;
- limitations;
- security considerations;
- expected behaviour.

Documentation belongs to governance.

---

# Operational Responsibility

Platform teams should understand:

- what exists;
- why it exists;
- how it behaves;
- how it evolves;
- how it fails.

Operational knowledge should never depend on one individual.

---

# Product Rules

Artificial Intelligence remains governed.

Automation remains the primary execution mechanism.

Capabilities remain reusable.

Providers remain replaceable.

Business behaviour remains deterministic.

Governance remains centralized.

---

# Relationship With AI Strategy

AI Strategy defines platform vision.

AI Governance protects that vision.

---

# Relationship With Automation

Automation governs execution.

AI Governance governs intelligent capabilities.

Responsibilities remain separated.

---

# Relationship With Platform Governance

AI Governance extends Platform Governance.

It never replaces it.

---

# Future Evolution

Future governance capabilities may include:

- capability certification;
- AI policy engines;
- automated governance checks;
- risk scoring;
- governance dashboards.

These capabilities should preserve architectural simplicity.

---

# Success Criteria

AI Governance is successful when:

- AI remains aligned with platform principles;
- provider replacement remains simple;
- deterministic behaviour is preserved;
- AI evolution remains controlled;
- governance remains transparent.

---

# Conclusion

AI Governance protects the evolution of Artificial Intelligence across Life Community OS.

Automation remains the foundation.

Artificial Intelligence remains a governed platform capability.

---

*"Artificial Intelligence evolves. Governance keeps it aligned with the platform."*
