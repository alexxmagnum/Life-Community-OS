# 11_CONVERSATION

Version: 1.0
Status: Draft
Document Type: Product Specification
Priority: Critical

---

# Purpose

This document defines the concept of a Conversation within Life Community OS.

A Conversation represents a structured communication channel between People and Entities regarding a specific context.

Conversations facilitate coordination.

They are never the purpose of the platform.

The purpose is participation.

---

# Question this document answers

> What is a Conversation?

---

# Scope

This specification defines the product behaviour of Conversations.

It describes:

- communication;
- participants;
- context;
- relationships;
- lifecycle.

It does not define:

- messaging protocols;
- push notifications;
- email;
- implementation details.

---

# Definition

A Conversation is a contextual communication channel.

Every Conversation exists because there is a reason to communicate.

Communication should always have context.

The platform should avoid isolated conversations without purpose whenever possible.

---

# Examples

Examples include:

- Experience Chat
- Community Project Discussion
- Marketplace Listing Conversation
- Mobility Coordination
- Reservation Discussion
- Support Conversation
- Private Conversation
- Organization Communication
- Volunteer Coordination

The same Conversation model supports all of them.

---

# A Conversation Is Not a Notification

A Notification informs.

A Conversation communicates.

Notifications may lead to Conversations.

They remain independent concepts.

---

# A Conversation Is Not a Community Project

Projects generate Conversations.

Conversations support Projects.

The Project remains the primary concept.

---

# A Conversation Is Not an Experience

Experiences may generate Conversations.

The Conversation exists to support participation.

It does not define the Experience.

---

# Responsibilities

A Conversation is responsible for:

- enabling communication;
- preserving context;
- connecting participants;
- maintaining communication history.

Nothing more.

---

# Context

Every Conversation should belong to a context whenever possible.

Examples include:

Experience

Marketplace Listing

Mobility Offer

Community Project

Entity

Support Request

Private Conversation

Context improves clarity.

---

# Participants

Participants may include:

- People;
- Entities (represented by People);
- administrators;
- moderators.

Participation may evolve over time.

---

# Visibility

A Conversation may be:

- private;
- group;
- organization;
- community;
- context-restricted.

Visibility depends on its purpose.

---

# History

Conversations preserve communication history.

History supports:

- continuity;
- transparency;
- collaboration.

Retention policies belong to the Data Model.

---

# Moderation

Conversations may be moderated.

Moderation should protect respectful participation.

It should never discourage healthy discussion.

---

# Relationships

Conversations may relate to:

- People
- Entities
- Experiences
- Community Projects
- Marketplace Listings
- Mobility Offers
- Notifications
- Media

---

# Product Rules

Every Conversation has a purpose.

Whenever possible, Conversations should belong to a context.

Participants may join or leave.

History should remain preserved according to platform policies.

Conversations should encourage collaboration rather than distraction.

---

# Lifecycle

Typical lifecycle:

Created

↓

Active

↓

Inactive

↓

Archived

Conversations may also be Closed when appropriate.

---

# Future Evolution

Future versions may support:

- AI summaries;
- automatic translation;
- voice conversations;
- video conversations;
- threaded discussions;
- polls;
- collaborative notes.

These additions should preserve the conceptual definition.

---

# Future Implications

This specification directly influences:

- Notifications
- AI
- Search
- Discovery
- Moderation
- Governance
- Accessibility
- Analytics

---

# Success Criteria

The Conversation specification is successful when:

- communication always has context;
- collaboration remains simple;
- history is preserved;
- moderation supports healthy participation;
- future communication channels reuse the same Conversation model.

---

# Conclusion

Conversations enable People to communicate around shared activities, projects and community life.

Communication is most valuable when it is contextual, purposeful and respectful.

Life Community OS treats Conversations as collaborative tools rather than isolated chat systems.

---

*"Good conversations strengthen communities because they connect people around shared purposes, not just shared messages."*
