# 01_TERRITORY

Version: 1.0
Status: Draft
Document Type: Product Specification
Priority: Critical

---

# Purpose

This document defines the concept of a Territory within Life Community OS.

A Territory represents the primary geographical context in which a community exists, interacts and evolves.

It provides the physical framework that connects people, organizations, places, experiences and resources.

The Territory is not the community itself.

It is the environment where the community develops.

---

# Question this document answers

> What is a Territory?

---

# Scope

This specification defines the product behaviour of Territories.

It describes:

- what a Territory represents;
- its responsibilities;
- its relationships with other product concepts;
- its lifecycle.

It does not define:

- administrative boundaries;
- legal jurisdictions;
- database schemas;
- technical implementation.

---

# Definition

A Territory is a real-world geographical context recognized by the platform.

Examples include:

- residential communities;
- urbanizations;
- villages;
- neighbourhoods;
- towns;
- resorts;
- campuses;
- marinas;
- business parks;
- sports complexes.

The Territory provides geographical identity.

It does not define social identity.

---

# A Territory Is Not a Community

A Territory and a Community are different concepts.

A Territory represents where interactions happen.

A Community represents who participates.

A Territory may contain multiple communities.

A Community may evolve within one Territory.

Future platform evolution may allow Communities without a permanent Territory.

---

# Responsibilities

A Territory is responsible for:

- providing geographical context;
- grouping local places;
- grouping local entities;
- grouping local experiences;
- grouping local resources;
- supporting local discovery;
- enabling local participation.

Nothing more.

---

# Territory Identity

Every Territory has its own identity.

Examples include:

- name;
- description;
- imagery;
- geographical boundaries;
- culture;
- traditions;
- language diversity.

The platform should preserve this identity.

It should never standardize Territories.

---

# Territory Does Not Own People

People are never owned by a Territory.

People may belong to:

- one Territory;
- multiple Territories;
- no Territory.

Belonging is defined by Membership.

Not by Territory.

---

# Territory Does Not Own Organizations

Organizations remain independent.

They simply operate within one or more Territories.

The Territory provides context.

Not ownership.

---

# Territory Enables Discovery

Discovery should primarily occur within Territory context.

Examples:

Nearby experiences.

Local businesses.

Neighbour projects.

Community announcements.

Volunteer opportunities.

Shared mobility.

The Territory improves relevance.

---

# Territory Supports Local Identity

Each Territory may preserve:

- customs;
- traditions;
- local celebrations;
- preferred languages;
- visual identity;
- governance style.

The platform should strengthen local identity.

Never replace it.

---

# Territory Evolves

Territories evolve naturally.

Organizations appear.

People move.

Experiences change.

Projects finish.

New resources emerge.

The Territory remains stable while its ecosystem evolves continuously.

---

# Relationships

A Territory may contain:

- Places;
- Entities;
- Resources;
- Experiences;
- Community Projects.

People participate within Territories through Membership.

Organizations participate through Entities.

Experiences occur at Places.

Resources belong to Places or Entities.

---

# Product Rules

A Territory should never become a container for every platform concept.

Only concepts with geographical meaning should belong directly to a Territory.

Concepts without geographical context should remain independent.

---

# Future Evolution

Future versions may support:

- nested Territories;
- regional hierarchies;
- shared Territories;
- virtual Territories;
- cross-border Territories.

These extensions should not require changing the conceptual definition.

---

# Future Implications

This specification directly influences:

- Membership
- Place
- Entity
- Resource
- Experience
- Community Project
- Discovery
- Mobility
- Search
- Notifications

---

# Success Criteria

The Territory specification is successful when:

- geographical context remains independent from social participation;
- local identity is preserved;
- discovery becomes geographically relevant;
- future expansion remains possible;
- Territories remain reusable across different community types.

---

# Conclusion

A Territory is the geographical foundation of Life Community OS.

It provides location, context and identity without defining ownership, authority or participation.

Communities live within Territories.

Territories enable communities to flourish.
