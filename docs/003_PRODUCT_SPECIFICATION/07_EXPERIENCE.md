Version: 1.0

# Purpose

This document defines the concept of an Experience within Life Community OS.

An Experience represents any organized activity that brings People together around a shared purpose.

Experiences are one of the primary ways communities create value.

They transform Places, Resources and People into real participation.

---

# Question this document answers

> What is an Experience?

---

# Scope

This specification defines the product behaviour of Experiences.

It describes:

- organized activities;
- participation;
- relationships;
- lifecycle.

It does not define:

- reservations;
- ticketing;
- payments;
- implementation.

---

# Definition

An Experience is any planned activity involving one or more participants.

Experiences exist to create real-world interaction.

An Experience may involve:

learning;

sports;

culture;

community;

business;

entertainment;

volunteering;

wellbeing.

The platform does not distinguish between these categories at the conceptual level.

---

Examples include:

- Golf Tournament
- Yoga Class
- Tennis Lesson
- Community Meeting
- Charity Dinner
- Volunteer Day
- Beach Cleanup
- Live Concert
- Children's Workshop
- Business Networking Event
- Farmers Market

- Guided Tour
- Language Exchange
- Book Club
- Board Game Night

The same Experience model supports all of them.

---

# An Experience Is Not a Place

Experiences happen at Places.

The Place provides location.

The Experience provides purpose.

---

# An Experience Is Not a Resource

Experiences may use Resources.

Resources remain independent.

Experiences never own Resources.

---

# An Experience Is Not an Entity

Entities may organize Experiences.

Experiences remain independent.

Changing the organizer does not redefine the Experience.

---

# An Experience Is Not a Community Project

Experiences are temporary.

Community Projects pursue longer-term objectives.

A Community Project may generate many Experiences.

---

# Responsibilities

An Experience is responsible for:

- bringing People together;
- defining participation;
- defining purpose;
- defining schedule;
- connecting Places;
- optionally using Resources;
- creating community value.

Nothing more.

---

# Participation

Experiences exist because People participate.

Participation may include:

- organizer;
- host;
- instructor;
- volunteer;
- participant;
- guest;
- spectator.

Participation should remain flexible.

---

# Organization

Experiences may be organized by:

- a Person;
- an Entity;
- multiple Entities;
- a Community Project.

The Experience remains the same regardless of who organizes it.

---

# Location

An Experience may:

occur at one Place;

span multiple Places;

be distributed across several locations;

or, in future versions, exist entirely online.

Location supports the Experience.

It does not define it.

---

# Resources

Experiences may use zero, one or many Resources.

Resources remain optional.

Simple Experiences should not require Resources.

The platform should never force unnecessary complexity.

---

# Capacity

Experiences may define participation limits.

Examples include:

- maximum attendees;
- waiting list;
- registration deadline;
- participant categories.

Capacity belongs to the Experience.

Not to the Place.

---

# Discovery

Experiences should be easy to discover.

Relevant criteria may include:

- Territory;
- Membership;
- interests;
- language;
- proximity;
- recommendations.

Discovery increases participation.

---

# Community Impact

Every Experience should contribute positively to community life.

Examples include:

- learning;
- social interaction;
- wellbeing;
- collaboration;
- local economy;
- volunteering;
- cultural exchange.

Experiences exist to strengthen communities.

---

# Relationships

Experiences may relate to:

- People
- Territories
- Places
- Resources
- Entities
- Community Projects
- Conversations
- Media
- Notifications

Relationships evolve independently.

---

# Product Rules

Every Experience has a purpose.

Experiences may exist without Resources.

Experiences should remain independent from reservations.

Experiences should never depend on a specific business type.

The same Experience model should support every activity.

---

# Lifecycle

Typical lifecycle:

Draft

↓

Scheduled

↓

Published

↓

Open

↓

In Progress

↓

Completed

↓

Archived

Cancellation may occur at any stage before completion.

---

# Future Evolution

Future versions may support:

- hybrid Experiences;
- recurring Experiences;
- AI-assisted organization;
- collaborative Experiences;
- automatically generated agendas;
- smart participant matching;
- live telemetry.

These extensions should preserve the conceptual definition.

---

# Future Implications

This specification directly influences:

- Reservations
- Discovery
- Search
- Marketplace
- Community Projects
- Notifications
- Automation
- AI
- Analytics
- Reputation

---

# Success Criteria

The Experience specification is successful when:

- every organized activity fits the same model;
- Experiences remain independent from business type;
- Resources remain optional;
- participation remains flexible;
- new activity types require configuration rather than redesign.

---

# Conclusion

Experiences are the heartbeat of Life Community OS.

They transform digital organization into real human interaction.

By treating every activity as the same conceptual model, the platform remains simple, scalable and adaptable to every kind of community.
