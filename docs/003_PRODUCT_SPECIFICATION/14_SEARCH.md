# 14_SEARCH

Version: 1.0
Status: Draft
Document Type: Product Specification
Priority: Critical

---

# Purpose

This document defines the concept of Search within Life Community OS.

Search represents the capability of locating relevant information across the platform quickly, accurately and contextually.

The objective is not searching.

The objective is finding.

---

# Question this document answers

> How do People find what they need?

---

# Scope

This specification defines the product behaviour of Search.

It describes:

- information retrieval;
- contextual search;
- relationships;
- lifecycle.

It does not define:

- search engines;
- indexing technologies;
- databases;
- implementation.

---

# Definition

Search is the capability that allows People to locate relevant information regardless of where it exists within the platform.

Search should feel natural.

People should think about what they need.

Not where it is stored.

---

# Searchable Content

Search may include:

- People
- Entities
- Places
- Experiences
- Community Projects
- Marketplace Listings
- Mobility Offers
- Conversations
- Media
- Documents
- Resources

Future concepts should automatically become searchable whenever appropriate.

---

# Search Is Not Discovery

Search answers an explicit intention.

The user knows what they are looking for.

Discovery helps users find things they were not actively searching for.

Both concepts complement each other.

---

# Responsibilities

Search is responsible for:

- locating information;
- ranking results;
- understanding context;
- reducing search effort.

Nothing more.

---

# Context

Search should understand context.

Examples include:

Current Territory

Current Membership

Preferred Language

Recent Activity

Permissions

Availability

Context improves relevance.

---

# Relevance

Search should prioritize:

- exact matches;
- contextual matches;
- semantic similarity;
- popularity;
- proximity;
- freshness.

Relevance should continuously improve.

---

# Filters

Search may support filters such as:

- category;
- Territory;
- Entity;
- date;
- availability;
- language;
- distance;
- status.

Filtering refines.

It should never replace good ranking.

---

# Permissions

Search must respect platform permissions.

People should never discover information they are not authorized to access.

Security precedes discoverability.

---

# Multilingual Search

Search should work independently of language whenever possible.

Equivalent concepts published in different languages should remain discoverable.

Language should never become a barrier.

---

# Artificial Intelligence

Artificial Intelligence may improve Search through:

- semantic understanding;
- typo correction;
- intent recognition;
- query expansion;
- result summarization.

AI enhances Search.

It does not replace it.

---

# Relationships

Search may relate to:

- People
- Entities
- Places
- Experiences
- Community Projects
- Marketplace Listings
- Mobility Offers
- Conversations
- Media
- Notifications

---

# Product Rules

Every searchable concept should define searchable information.

Search should always respect permissions.

Search should minimize user effort.

Results should remain explainable whenever possible.

---

# Lifecycle

Typical lifecycle:

Query

↓

Processing

↓

Results

↓

Refinement

↓

Selection

↓

Completion

Every search contributes to improving future relevance.

---

# Future Evolution

Future versions may support:

- natural language search;
- voice search;
- image search;
- AI-generated answers;
- predictive search;
- cross-community search.

These additions should preserve the conceptual definition.

---

# Future Implications

This specification directly influences:

- Discovery
- AI
- Accessibility
- Multilanguage
- Performance
- Analytics
- User Experience

---

# Success Criteria

The Search specification is successful when:

- users quickly find what they need;
- permissions are always respected;
- relevance improves over time;
- multilingual content remains discoverable;
- new platform concepts become searchable without redesign.

---

# Conclusion

Search helps People locate the right information with the least possible effort.

Rather than exposing the internal structure of the platform, Search should understand user intent and provide relevant, secure and contextual results.

---

*"People should remember what they want—not where it is stored."*
