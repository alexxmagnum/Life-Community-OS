# 09_MARKETPLACE_LISTING

Version: 1.0
Status: Draft
Document Type: Product Specification
Priority: Critical

---

# Purpose

This document defines the concept of a Marketplace Listing within Life Community OS.

A Marketplace Listing represents any public offer, request or exchange published by a Person or Entity for the benefit of the community.

Listings facilitate local commerce, collaboration and resource sharing.

The Marketplace is not only about selling.

It is about enabling exchanges.

---

# Question this document answers

> What is a Marketplace Listing?

---

# Scope

This specification defines the product behaviour of Marketplace Listings.

It describes:

- offers;
- requests;
- exchanges;
- relationships;
- lifecycle.

It does not define:

- payments;
- logistics;
- messaging implementation;
- commerce regulations.

---

# Definition

A Marketplace Listing is a public publication describing something that another participant may interact with.

A Listing may represent:

- something offered;
- something requested;
- something exchanged;
- something donated;
- something rented;
- something borrowed.

The platform treats every Listing through the same conceptual model.

---

# Examples

Examples include:

- Sell a Bicycle
- Buy Golf Clubs
- Rent an Apartment
- Offer Gardening Services
- Need a Babysitter
- Give Away Furniture
- Exchange Books
- Share Firewood
- Looking for a Tennis Partner
- Offer Piano Lessons
- Lend a Ladder
- Sell Homemade Cakes

The same Listing model supports all of them.

---

# A Marketplace Listing Is Not a Person

People create Listings.

Listings remain independent.

Changing ownership should not redefine the Listing.

---

# A Marketplace Listing Is Not an Entity

Entities may publish Listings.

People may publish Listings.

The Listing remains the same.

---

# A Marketplace Listing Is Not a Community Project

Projects pursue shared objectives.

Listings facilitate exchanges.

They solve different problems.

---

# Responsibilities

A Marketplace Listing is responsible for:

- describing an offer or request;
- defining its availability;
- enabling discovery;
- connecting interested participants;
- supporting local exchange.

Nothing more.

---

# Publisher

A Listing may be published by:

- a Person;
- an Entity.

The publisher remains responsible for the Listing.

---

# Types

Examples include:

- Sale
- Purchase
- Rental
- Loan
- Exchange
- Donation
- Service
- Wanted
- Collaboration

The platform should allow future Listing types without redesign.

---

# Availability

A Listing may become:

Draft

↓

Published

↓

Paused

↓

Reserved

↓

Completed

↓

Archived

It may also be Cancelled or Expired.

---

# Visibility

Listings may define visibility rules.

Examples include:

- entire Territory;
- verified Members only;
- selected Communities;
- public visitors (where appropriate).

Visibility should remain configurable.

---

# Trust

Marketplace Listings should encourage trust.

Examples include:

- verified Membership;
- publisher reputation;
- community history;
- profile completeness.

Trust assists decisions.

It should never become exclusion.

---

# Discovery

Listings should be discoverable through:

- category;
- proximity;
- Territory;
- language;
- interests;
- recommendations;
- availability.

Discovery should prioritize relevance.

---

# Relationships

Marketplace Listings may relate to:

- People
- Entities
- Territories
- Conversations
- Media
- Notifications

Optionally:

- Community Projects
- Experiences

---

# Product Rules

Every Listing has exactly one publisher.

Listings represent exchanges.

Not transactions.

Payments are optional.

Listings should remain independent from payment providers.

---

# Future Evolution

Future versions may support:

- escrow;
- shipping integrations;
- AI-assisted descriptions;
- fraud detection;
- price recommendations;
- automatic categorization.

These additions should preserve the conceptual definition.

---

# Future Implications

This specification directly influences:

- Search
- Discovery
- Conversations
- Notifications
- Trust
- AI
- Analytics

---

# Success Criteria

The Marketplace Listing specification is successful when:

- every local exchange fits the same model;
- commerce remains independent from payment systems;
- listings remain reusable;
- trust increases participation;
- future commerce capabilities require configuration rather than redesign.

---

# Conclusion

Marketplace Listings represent opportunities for exchange within the community.

Whether selling, buying, lending, donating or requesting, every Listing exists to connect People and Entities through local participation.

The Marketplace is not the goal.

Community exchange is.
