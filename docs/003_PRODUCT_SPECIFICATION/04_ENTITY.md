# Purpose

This document defines the concept of an Entity within Life Community OS.

An Entity represents any organized participant capable of interacting with the community.

Entities contribute services, resources, experiences, projects and value to the ecosystem.

Entities are independent from the people who manage them.

---

# Question this document answers

> What is an Entity?

---

# Scope

This specification defines the product behaviour of Entities.

It describes:

- organizational identity;
- responsibilities;
- participation;
- relationships;
- lifecycle.

It does not define:

- legal entities;
- authentication;
- permissions;
- accounting;
- technical implementation.

---

# Definition

An Entity is an organized participant within the platform.

An Entity represents an organization rather than a person.

Entities participate in community life through the people who represent them.

# An Entity Is Not a Person

People own, represent or work for Entities.

The Entity exists independently.

Changing representatives should never change the Entity itself.

---

# An Entity Is Not a Membership

Membership belongs to People.

Entities may participate in communities.

They do not possess Membership in the same way a Person does.

---

# An Entity Is Not a Place

An Entity may operate:

- at one Place;
- at many Places;
- remotely;
- across multiple Territories.

Location does not define the Entity.

---

# Responsibilities

An Entity may:

- organize Experiences;
- manage Places;
- manage Resources;
- publish Announcements;
- create Community Projects;
- offer Marketplace services;
- collaborate with other Entities;
- participate in community initiatives.

Nothing more.

---

# Representation

Every Entity is represented by one or more People.

Representation is explicit.

People may gain or lose representation.

The Entity remains unchanged.

---

# Identity

Every Entity has its own identity.

Examples include:

- name;
- description;
- category;
- visual identity;
- contact information;
- supported languages;
- operating Territories.

Identity belongs to the Entity.

Not to its representatives.

---

# Participation

Entities enrich community life.

They provide:

- services;
- activities;
- resources;
- knowledge;
- employment;
- collaboration;
- sponsorship;
- support.

The platform should encourage active participation by Entities.

# Relationships

Entities may relate to:

- People
- Territories
- Places
- Resources
- Experiences
- Community Projects
- Marketplace Listings
- Conversations
- Media
- Notifications

Relationships evolve independently.

---

# Product Rules

An Entity always represents an organization.

Entities never replace People.

Entities never authenticate.

Entities act through authorized representatives.

An Entity may exist before having public activity.

---

# Future Evolution

Future versions may support:

- federations of Entities;
- parent-child Entities;
- verified Entities;
- public/private Entities;
- temporary Entities;
- digital-only Entities.

These extensions should preserve the conceptual definition.

---

# Future Implications

This specification directly influences:

- Places
- Resources
- Experiences
- Marketplace
- Community Projects
- Administration
- Governance
- Discovery
- Search
- Notifications

---

# Success Criteria

The Entity specification is successful when:

- every organization is represented through a common model;
- organizations remain independent from People;
- collaboration is encouraged;
- future Entity types require configuration rather than redesign;
- the same Entity model supports public, private and community organizations.

---

# Conclusion

Entities represent the organized participants of Life Community OS.

They provide structure, services and collaboration while remaining independent from the people who represent them.

A strong ecosystem depends not only on active People, but also on active Entities working together.
