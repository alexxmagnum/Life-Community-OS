# 03_MEMBERSHIP

Version: 1.0
Status: Draft
Document Type: Product Specification
Priority: Critical

---

# Purpose

This document defines the concept of Membership within Life Community OS.

A Membership describes the relationship between a Person and a Territory or Community.

It defines how someone belongs.

It never defines who someone is.

Membership is dynamic.

Identity is permanent.

---

# Question this document answers

> How does a Person belong to a Territory or Community?

---

# Scope

This specification defines the product behaviour of Membership.

It describes:

- belonging;
- participation context;
- relationship lifecycle;
- responsibilities.

It does not define:

- authentication;
- permissions;
- roles;
- reputation;
- technical implementation.

---

# Definition

A Membership represents the relationship between a Person and a Community within a specific Territory.

It describes belonging.

Nothing more.

Membership exists because communities need context.

People exist independently from Membership.

---

# Membership Is Not Identity

Identity answers:

Who are you?

Membership answers:

How do you belong?

Identity never changes because Membership changes.

---

# Membership Is Not Permission

Membership does not authorize actions.

Permissions belong to the authorization model.

Two members with the same Membership may have different permissions.

---

# Membership Is Not a Role

Roles describe responsibilities.

Membership describes belonging.

Examples:

Resident

↓

Membership

Community Moderator

↓

Role

These concepts must remain independent.

---

# Membership Is Contextual

Membership always belongs to a context.

Examples:

A residential community.

A sports club.

A resort.

A marina.

A campus.

A neighbourhood.

Without context, Membership has no meaning.

---

# Membership Evolves

Membership may begin.

Membership may change.

Membership may end.

The Person remains unchanged.

Communities evolve naturally.

Membership reflects this evolution.

---

# Responsibilities

Membership is responsible for:

- expressing belonging;
- defining participation context;
- connecting People with Communities;
- supporting community life.

Nothing else.

---

# Types of Belonging

Examples may include:

- Resident
- Property Owner
- Tenant
- Employee
- Volunteer
- Student
- Guest
- Visitor
- Seasonal Resident
- Business Representative

The platform should remain extensible.

Membership types are configurable.

Not hardcoded.

---

# One Person, Multiple Memberships

A Person may belong to:

- multiple Territories;
- multiple Communities;
- multiple Organizations.

Every Membership remains independent.

Participation is contextual.

---

# Membership and Organizations

Organizations may require Membership.

Membership does not create Organizations.

Organizations do not own Membership.

They simply relate to it.

---

# Membership Supports Trust

Membership contributes to community trust.

Belonging may strengthen confidence.

It should never become exclusion.

The platform should remain welcoming while protecting community value.

---

# Membership Supports Discovery

Relevant content should consider Membership.

Examples include:

local experiences;

community projects;

announcements;

resources;

services;

volunteer opportunities.

Membership improves relevance.

It should never isolate users.

---

# Relationships

Membership relates to:

- Person
- Territory
- Entity
- Experience
- Community Project
- Marketplace
- Mobility
- Conversation

Membership connects these concepts through participation.

---

# Product Rules

Every Membership belongs to exactly one Person.

A Membership always belongs to a specific context.

Membership never owns a Person.

Membership never defines permissions.

Membership never replaces identity.

---

# Future Evolution

Future versions may support:

- temporary Memberships;
- invitation-based Memberships;
- verified Memberships;
- organization-sponsored Memberships;
- seasonal Memberships;
- digital-only Communities.

These additions should extend the model without changing its conceptual definition.

---

# Future Implications

This specification directly influences:

- Identity
- Trust
- Governance
- Experiences
- Marketplace
- Mobility
- Community Projects
- Organizations
- Discovery
- Notifications

---

# Success Criteria

The Membership specification is successful when:

- belonging remains independent from identity;
- permissions remain separate;
- communities support multiple Membership types;
- participation remains flexible;
- new Membership models require configuration rather than redesign.

---

# Conclusion

Membership defines belonging.

It is the bridge between People and Communities.

By keeping Membership independent from Identity, Roles and Permissions, Life Community OS remains flexible enough to support many kinds of communities while preserving a simple conceptual model.

---

*"Identity tells us who a Person is. Membership tells us where that Person belongs."*
