# 07_IDENTITY_AND_TRUST

**Version:** 1.0
**Status:** Draft
**Document Type:** Constitution
**Priority:** Critical

---

# Purpose

This document defines the constitutional model for Identity, Membership and Trust within Life Community OS.

Identity answers who someone is.

Membership answers how someone belongs.

Permissions answer what someone may do.

Trust answers how confidence is established.

These concepts are intentionally independent and must never be merged.

---

# Question this document answers

> **How does Life Community OS identify people, establish belonging and build trust?**

---

# Scope

This document defines conceptual behaviour.

It does not define:

* authentication providers;
* login implementation;
* security protocols;
* database models.

---

# Identity Is Permanent

A Person has one identity.

Identity does not depend on:

* residence;
* employment;
* ownership;
* membership;
* permissions.

People may change their relationship with a Territory.

Their identity remains the same.

---

# Membership Defines Belonging

Membership describes how a Person belongs to a Territory or Entity.

Examples include:

* Resident
* Property Owner
* Tenant
* Employee
* Volunteer
* Student
* Visitor
* Business Representative

Membership may change over time.

Identity does not.

---

# Roles Define Responsibilities

Roles describe responsibilities.

Examples:

* Community Moderator
* Restaurant Manager
* School Administrator
* Sports Coordinator
* Marketplace Moderator

Roles are assigned.

They are never inferred from identity.

---

# Permissions Define Actions

Permissions determine what someone can do.

Examples:

* Create Experiences
* Publish Announcements
* Moderate Comments
* Manage Resources
* Approve Reservations

Permissions never define status.

---

# Trust Is Earned

Trust is built through consistent participation.

The platform should encourage trustworthy behaviour.

Trust should never depend on popularity.

---

# Trust Comes From Multiple Signals

Confidence may grow through:

* verified relationships;
* positive participation;
* completed activities;
* community contributions;
* responsible behaviour;
* transparent moderation.

No single signal should determine trust.

---

# Identity Should Never Create Privilege

People should not receive platform privileges simply because of:

* ownership;
* wealth;
* nationality;
* language;
* age.

Responsibilities may differ.

Human value does not.

---

# Verification Is Progressive

The platform should support different confidence levels.

Examples include:

* Basic identity
* Confirmed membership
* Verified organization representative
* Verified community administrator

Verification should increase trust without creating unnecessary barriers.

---

# Privacy Is a Fundamental Right

Users should control the visibility of their personal information whenever possible.

Only information required for platform functionality should be mandatory.

The platform should always minimize unnecessary personal data exposure.

---

# Reputation Is Contextual

A person's reputation may vary depending on context.

Someone may be:

* an excellent volunteer;
* a reliable marketplace seller;
* an active sports organizer.

Reputation should reflect contribution within each context rather than a single global score.

---

# Participation Builds Trust

The strongest form of trust comes from meaningful participation.

Helping others.

Creating experiences.

Supporting initiatives.

Contributing knowledge.

Building the community.

The platform should recognize participation naturally.

---

# Organizations Build Institutional Trust

Entities also establish trust.

Consistency.

Transparency.

Reliable information.

Quality experiences.

Responsible management.

Organizations should earn trust through behaviour rather than labels.

---

# Trust Should Be Explainable

Whenever trust influences platform behaviour, users should understand why.

Opaque trust systems reduce confidence.

Transparency strengthens trust.

---

# Belonging Evolves

People may belong to multiple Territories during their lives.

Membership should support this evolution naturally.

Belonging is dynamic.

Identity remains stable.

---

# The Platform Protects Trust

Every capability should reinforce community confidence.

Examples include:

* preventing abuse;
* encouraging respectful interactions;
* reducing misinformation;
* making moderation accountable.

Trust is considered a shared responsibility.

---

# Success Criteria

The Identity and Trust Constitution is successful when:

* people feel safe participating;
* organizations earn confidence through actions;
* permissions remain understandable;
* belonging feels natural;
* trust increases over time through participation.

---

# Future Implications

This document directly influences:

* Authentication
* Membership Engine
* Permission System
* Governance

* Reputation
* Notifications

---

# Conclusion

Identity defines who people are.

Membership defines where they belong.

Permissions define what they can do.

Trust defines how communities grow.

Keeping these concepts independent ensures that Life Community OS remains inclusive, scalable and fair while strengthening real community relationships.

---

*"Communities are strengthened not by status, but by trust earned through participation."*
